# Cartographic art direction

The visual constitution for the map (V2 §1). Every later rendering decision cites
this document or argues with it in review; nothing visual ships on taste alone.
The reference renders live in `docs/reference/` and are regenerated by
`scripts/regenerate_goldens.py`; the corpus they are drawn from is `tests/corpus.py`.

**The target**: a modern relief atlas crossed with a well-made historical political
map. Printed-paper calm, real hillshading, typographic discipline. NOT parchment,
NOT burns and stains, NOT a fantasy pastiche. A reader should half-remember a school
atlas, not a prop.

**The one principle** (the brief's own): *nothing should look placed merely because
the world model says it exists.* Every visible thing should look as though
geography, history, human behaviour and a cartographer jointly determined why it
appears where and how it does.

## The four reads

A finished map answers four questions in order, at a glance, before any legend:

1. **Where is the land, and what shape is it?** Coast and relief dominate every
   other signal. If politics reads before terrain, the compositing is wrong.
2. **How does water move?** Ridges, valleys, rivers to the sea. The drainage should
   be legible as a *system* — you can trace a drop of rain.
3. **Where do people live, and how do they move?** Settlement hierarchy and the
   road/lane network, read by size, shape and weight before colour.
4. **Whose is it?** Political tint, borders, holders — the *last* read in atlas
   mode, the first only in analytical mode.

## Line hierarchy

Heaviest to lightest, and never re-ordered:

    coastline > border > river trunk > highway > river tributary > road > track > contour/grain

- The coastline is the strongest line on the map. A political border that coincides
  with the coast is NOT drawn — the coast wins (the double-stroke is a defect).
- Rivers taper: a trunk is visibly wider than its tributaries, and width grows only
  downstream. Equal-width rivers are the plainest generated tell.
- Roads are warm-toned and thinner than rivers at equal rank; a road never
  outweighs the water it crosses.

## Type hierarchy

One serif voice today; the full voice set arrives in Phase D and is specified here
so it lands as design rather than accretion:

| kind | face & case | notes |
|---|---|---|
| seas, large waters | italic, generous tracking | may curve; the water's own colour |
| regions / land areas | letterspaced small caps | tracking ≥ 0.12em, never bold |
| mountain ranges | small caps along the ridge | follows the spine |
| rivers | italic along the flow | reads downstream |
| settlements | upright, weight by rank | capital > city > town > village |
| roads, small features | smallest, sparing | only at close zoom |

Every label carries a halo of the paper colour (`--map-halo`); halo weight may vary
with ground texture but never disappears. Label sizes are set by the server
(`cartography.TYPE`); the stylesheet sets the voice.

## Texture and restraint

Texture is *earned*: it appears where the model has something to say and goes quiet
where it does not. "Empty terrain is important — resist filling every space."

- Relief detail follows drainage: carved valley country may be busy; plains and
  lowlands go quiet. Uniform noise everywhere is the defect this line exists for.
- The sea is a surface, not a fill: up to three depth bands, no waves, no ships.
- Paper grain, when it arrives (Phase E), stays under ±1% luminance. No parchment.

## Restraint budgets

Cited by the phase that implements each feature; changing a budget is an edit to
this file, reviewed as such.

| thing | budget |
|---|---|
| deltas per map | ≤ 2 |
| lakes per map | ≤ 3 |
| distributary arms per delta | 2–3 |
| coast character | one class per stretch; minimum run ≥ 12 shore cells before it may change |
| drawn rivers | trunks + order-≥2 tributaries only; brooks are relief, not features |
| crossings (bridge/ford glyphs) per river | ≤ 3 |
| label density | per 200×200-unit neighbourhood: ≤ 1 tier-0, ≤ 3 tier-1, ≤ 6 total |
| second labels (long rivers/ranges) | reach ≥ 55% of the map's span, one repeat |
| icon clearance | label boxes never overlap an icon + 2 units |
| negative space | ≥ 25% of land area carries no label or icon at world zoom |

## Zoom bands (Phase D)

Three bands, switched by the client's zoom factor `k` against the fitted view:

| band | k | shows |
|---|---|---|
| world | < 1.8 | coast, regions, seas, ranges, trunk rivers, capitals/cities, highways |
| regional | 1.8–3.5 | + towns, tributaries, castles, roads, large natural features |
| local | > 3.5 | + villages, crossings, tracks, settlement footprints, minor names |

Labels are solved per band on the server; a name dropped at world zoom appears in
the first band with room for it. Terrain never changes between bands — only what is
written on it.

## Relief philosophy

What `shade.py` already does right is codified, not re-litigated: printed-atlas
value band (no near-black shadows, no white blowouts — SHADE_FLOOR's range), key
light from the north-west, hypsometric tint under Lambertian shading, sea ramp
distinct from land ramp. Phase B adds carved drainage and coast-class shading;
Phase E adds the second light, valley ambient occlusion and height haze (haze ≤ 10%
of image height in effect). All of it stays byte-deterministic.

## Colour

Every colour is a role in `web/src/styles.css` (`--map-*`), with a light and a dark
value; Python emits role names only (guard-tested). Political holder colours are
palette slots (`holder-N`), stable per holder per world. Accessibility palettes
(Phase E) are full `--map-*` blocks under `data-palette`, not filters.

## Measured baselines (Phase A, world = Renn accepted with everything)

Recorded so later phases can claim improvement against numbers, not memory:

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| `plan_map` | 3.2 s |
| `/api/map` | 0.33 s, 122 KB |
| `cartography.draw()` | 0.34 s (uncached, every request) |
| labels | 51 placed, 36 dropped (41%), all "no room" |
| total feature vertices | 2 019 (budget 40 000) |
| relief render | cached per (branch, updated_at, scale) |

Targets by end of programme: tier-0/1 drop rate 0 at world band, ≥ 95% of names
placed across bands, draw() ≤ 1.2 s for three bands uncached and < 5 ms cached,
payload ≤ 300 KB on the `empire` corpus world.

## Reference renders

`docs/reference/relief-{renn,alps,delta,long_coast}.png` are the pinned Phase-A
ground renders (also hash-pinned in `tests/goldens/relief/`). Full-map renders come
from `scripts/render_map.py` — the parity renderer that draws through the real
`/api/map` + DrawPlan + stylesheet path. Judge changes by looking at before/after
pairs of these, in both themes, before re-pinning goldens.
